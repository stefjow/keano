# Is building on the Terrascope L3 monthly product sound, or should maeshle move to L2?

Discussion note, 2026-08-11. Conclusion up front: **stay on L3.** The choice
is not a convenience that L2 would fix — it is aligned with the system's
actual epistemics (monthly cadence, within-cell relative comparisons,
empirically calibrated noise gates, append-only history). Switching would
multiply storage and code by orders of magnitude and force a recalibration
of the credit rule, in exchange for coverage we should not want and one
genuinely valuable capability that is better built *beside* the L3 record
than instead of it.

## Why L3 fits

**The cadence argument is decisive.** Every metric — `m`, `perf_short`,
`perf_5y`, the credit baseline — is built on trailing 12-month means of
monthly values, deliberately (deseasonalization by construction, causality,
noise smoothing). Daily L2 granularity would be aggregated straight back to
monthly means before any metric touched it: terabytes of temporal
resolution downloaded in order to be thrown away.

**The one piece of L2 information that matters at hex scale is already
recovered.** `NO2_WEIGHT` is the per-pixel count of contributing L2
observations, and script 03 uses it for spatially weighted hex means — the
coverage effect measured to matter (p95 estimator difference 1.1% on
eligible cells, against the then-2% margin). Everything else L2 offers
(per-observation QA, cloud fractions, averaging kernels) is either applied
consistently upstream (qa > 0.75) or irrelevant to relative
month-over-month comparisons within a cell.

**The credit rule is calibrated to this product's noise.** The 1.2% margin
is the measured p95 `|Δlog m|` at lag 1 on flat-trend cells *of this L3
product*. A homemade L2-derived grid has different noise (own QA threshold,
own gridding kernel, own destriping or none), so the margin, the
plume-guard ramp and probably `NO2_FLOOR` would all need re-measuring. A
switch is not a data-source swap; it is a recalibration of the payment
rule.

**The append-only vintage contract is much easier to honor with L3.** One
GeoTIFF per month, fingerprinted in `vintage.csv`, hard stop on drift. With
L2 we would hold ~14 orbits/day × ~99 months of granules and — the bigger
problem — own the processor-version harmonization ourselves. S5P L2 NO₂ has
known discontinuities across processor versions (the 1.x → 2.x transition
around 2021 shifted tropospheric columns) and reprocessing campaigns since;
raw L2 means splicing OFFL and RPRO/PAL streams and managing the seams. For
a system whose core promise is "closed months never change", that is a
liability, not a feature. Terrascope's TM v2 collection hands us a
consistently reprocessed series.

**Scale.** ~100 monthly global GeoTIFFs at 0.02° versus tens of terabytes
of orbit files plus superobservation/gridding code we would write and
maintain — rebuilding what VITO already runs, less well validated.

## What L2 would genuinely buy, and why not take it

1. **A looser QA threshold for winter/high-latitude coverage.** The
   polar-night pain (`TREND_WINDOW_SLACK`, `MIN_MONTHS_IN_WINDOW`) traces
   to qa > 0.75 excluding cloudy and snow/ice scenes; qa > 0.5 would fill
   those months in. But those retrievals are excluded *because they are
   biased*. For a display map that is a defensible trade; for a system that
   pays out on 1.2% undercuts, trading bias for coverage is the wrong
   direction — it would credit albedo and cloud statistics.
2. **Attacking the transport problem properly.** The most scientifically
   interesting use of L2 is wind-rotation / wind-sector analysis (L2 +
   ERA5) to separate local emission changes from transport — the physical
   problem the res-4 plume guard approximates. But that is a research
   project with its own literature, and the guard is a measured, working
   mitigation (full weight on 63% of paid months; under credit_v3 a block
   is a deferral, not a forfeit). If maeshle ever needs to defend credits at
   money-at-stake rigor, this is where to invest — and even then as a
   *side channel* computed against the same L3 record, not a replacement
   of the base product.
3. **Latency.** L2 arrives within days; the monthly composite lags. Worth
   roughly nothing at monthly cadence.

## Honest weaknesses of the L3 choice (named, none argue for switching)

- **Single-supplier risk.** If VITO discontinues the collection, the
  product ends. Mitigation already in place: the archive is append-only
  and authoritative, so history is safe; a continuation series could be
  spliced using the overlap period. Nothing to do now; it is the tail
  risk.
- **Upstream reprocessing decisions are inherited.** The vintage manifest
  turns this from a silent hazard into a loud, human-decided event — about
  the best available posture — but the "keep old vintage vs. rebase"
  decision will eventually land on someone's desk, and L3 offers no third
  option.
- **0.02° regridding smoothing.** The L3 grid slightly oversamples the
  native L2 footprint (~3.5×5.5 km), so hex means carry some
  gridding-kernel smoothing across hex boundaries. Consistent over time,
  so it cancels in every relative metric; it would only matter for
  absolute-concentration claims per hex, which the %-based design avoids.

## Revisit condition

L2 becomes worth it only if maeshle's credits ever need to withstand
adversarial scrutiny of the plume guard. The right move at that point is
L2 + wind as a validation layer beside the L3 record, not a base-product
swap.
